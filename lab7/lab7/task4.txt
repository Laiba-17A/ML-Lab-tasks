Task 4 - Evaluation and Model Choice

Metrics were calculated on the held-out test set (154 samples: 100 negative, 54 positive). Metrics were
implemented manually (TP, TN, FP, FN -> accuracy, precision, recall, F1) for the scratch logistic model
and match scikit-learn exactly.

Model                  TN  FP  FN  TP  Accuracy  Precision  Recall  F1
Perceptron (Scratch)   74  26  22  32  0.6883    0.5517     0.5926  0.5714
Logistic (Scratch)     84  16  26  28  0.7273    0.6364     0.5185  0.5714
Perceptron (sklearn)   55  45  26  28  0.5390    0.3836     0.5185  0.4409
Logistic (sklearn)     84  16  26  28  0.7273    0.6364     0.5185  0.5714

Preferred model: Perceptron (Scratch).

In screening, a false negative (missing a diabetic patient) is more costly than a false alarm. So the
key metric is recall, and the key cell of the confusion matrix is FN.
- The scratch perceptron has the highest recall (0.5926) and the fewest false negatives (22). It finds
  32 of 54 positive patients, compared with 28 for the other three models.
- It does raise more false alarms (FP 26 against 16 for logistic), so precision and accuracy are lower.
  This is an acceptable trade-off because a false alarm only leads to a follow-up test.
- The logistic models have the best accuracy (0.7273), but choosing by accuracy would miss 4 more
  diabetic patients. Accuracy is also misleading here because the classes are imbalanced (65/35).
- The sklearn perceptron is the worst: low accuracy, low precision and no recall advantage.

Note: logistic regression outputs probabilities, so its recall can be raised by lowering the 0.5
threshold (e.g. 0.3). With a tuned threshold it would likely be the strongest choice, but at the default
threshold the scratch perceptron misses the fewest positive cases.
